🔗 Key Linkages — Why Net Income ≠ Cash

Net Income was $236M, but cash only increased by $141M. Why the $95M gap?
• +$50M Depreciation added back (non-cash expense reduced NI but didn't use cash)
• −$80M CapEx (real cash spent on equipment — never hits the income statement!)
• −$15M Working capital increase (sold on credit → revenue recognized but cash not yet received)
• −$30M Debt repayment (reduces debt on BS, no income statement impact)
• −$20M Dividends (paid to shareholders from retained earnings)
Free Cash Flow = $271 CFO − $80 CapEx = $191M — this is what matters for valuation.

📖 How to Read This

Each bar shows the valuation range from a different methodology. The red line is the current stock price.

• If current price is below most ranges → stock may be undervalued
• If current price sits in the middle → roughly fairly valued
• If current price is above most ranges → stock may be overvalued

The convergence zone — where multiple methods overlap — is where analysts have the most confidence in fair value.

Enterprise Value

What does this measure?

The total value of a business — what you'd pay to buy the entire company. Includes market cap (equity) PLUS debt, MINUS cash.

EV = Market Cap + Debt − Cash
